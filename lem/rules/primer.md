# Texas 42: Rules Primer (Straight Tournament)

Texas 42 is a trick-taking partnership game played with dominoes. This primer describes the straight tournament form of the game only. Anything not described here (Nel-O, Plunge, Splash, Sevens, regional variants) is out of scope.

## Players and equipment

The game uses one standard double-six domino set: 28 unique dominoes, each showing two halves with pip counts from 0 (blank) through 6. Four players play in two fixed partnerships. Partners sit across the table from each other, so play alternates between the two teams around the table.

Each hand, all 28 dominoes are shuffled face-down and each player draws 7. There is no boneyard; every domino is in some player's hand.

## Suits

A domino belongs to a "suit" based on its pips. The seven pip suits are blanks (0), ones, twos, threes, fours, fives, and sixes. A non-double domino belongs to the suit of each of its two halves (for example, the 5-2 is both a five and a two). A double belongs to the suit of its pips: 5-5 is a five, 6-6 is a six, and so on. In the natural ranking of a pip suit, the double of that suit is the highest member, followed by the other dominoes of that suit in descending order of the off-pip. For example, in the suit of fives (when fives are not trump): 5-5 is highest, then 6-5, 5-4, 5-3, 5-2, 5-1, 5-0.

## Bidding and trump declaration (abbreviated)

Before play, bidding occurs and one player wins the bid. The winning bidder names a trump declaration and leads the first trick. For the purposes of reasoning about play, the bid has already been resolved by the time any decision needs to be made: the contract value (in points from 30-41, or in marks where 1 mark = 42 points, 2 marks = 84, etc.), the bidding team, and the trump declaration are fixed.

The trump declaration is one of ten options, which reduce to three kinds:

1. A pip suit (blanks, ones, twos, threes, fours, fives, or sixes) is trump.
2. Doubles are trump.
3. No-trump.

## How trump reshapes the deck

When a pip suit is trump, every domino containing that pip is a trump, and those dominoes are no longer members of their other pip suit. The double of the trump suit is the highest trump. Example: with fours as trump, the trump suit ranked high to low is 4-4, 6-4, 5-4, 4-3, 4-2, 4-1, 4-0. The 6-4 is a trump and is no longer a six; if sixes are led on a later trick, 6-4 cannot follow because it is trump, not a six.

When doubles are trump, only the seven doubles are trump. They form a suit ranked 6-6 (high), 5-5, 4-4, 3-3, 2-2, 1-1, 0-0 (low). Non-double dominoes keep their natural pip suits but never contain trump. A double is no longer a member of its pip suit: when doubles are trump, 5-5 is a trump, not a five.

When no-trump is declared, there is no trump suit at all. All seven pip suits exist normally, doubles are the high member of their pip suit, and no domino can beat a domino of another suit. The highest domino of the led suit simply wins.

## The led suit

Each trick begins with a lead. The led domino determines the led suit by this rule:

1. If the led domino is a trump, the led suit is trump.
2. Otherwise, the led suit is the higher of the led domino's two pips.

This matters because a non-double, non-trump domino has two pips and could in principle belong to two suits, but the lead always commits it to its higher pip. Example: with twos as trump, leading 5-3 leads the suit of fives (the higher pip), not threes. Example: with fours as trump, leading 4-2 leads trump, not twos, because the 4-2 is a trump. Example: with doubles as trump, leading 5-5 leads trump; leading 6-3 leads the suit of sixes.

## Following suit

After the lead, the remaining three players in turn order must each play one domino, subject to this rule: a player holding any domino of the led suit must play one. A player holding no domino of the led suit may play any domino, including a trump.

Under doubles-as-trump, a double is trump and not a member of its pip suit, so doubles cannot be used to follow a non-double pip-suit lead. Conversely, when a double is led under doubles-as-trump, the led suit is trump and only other doubles are members of that suit; non-double dominoes cannot follow such a lead (and may be played only if the follower has no doubles at all).

## Winning the trick

All four plays to a trick are evaluated together:

1. If any trumps were played, the highest trump wins the trick.
2. Otherwise, the highest domino of the led suit wins. Dominoes of suits other than the led suit and trump cannot win a trick; they are off-suit discards.

Under no-trump, rule 1 never fires; the highest domino of the led suit always wins.

The winner of a trick leads the next trick. Seven tricks are played, exhausting all 28 dominoes.

## Counting and scoring a hand

There are exactly five count dominoes, worth 35 count points in total:

- 5-5 is worth 10 count points.
- 6-4 is worth 10 count points.
- 5-0 is worth 5 count points.
- 4-1 is worth 5 count points.
- 3-2 is worth 5 count points.

All other 23 dominoes are worth 0 count points. Count points belong to the team that wins the trick containing the count domino.

Each of the 7 tricks is also worth 1 trick point to the team that wins it. Total hand value: 35 count + 7 trick points = 42 points.

After all 7 tricks, each team's hand score is its count captured plus the number of tricks it won. The bidding team must take at least as many points as the bid to "make" the contract. If they do, they score the contract. If they fall short by any amount, they are "set" and the defending team scores the contract value instead.

A point bid of 30-41 is satisfied by taking at least that many of the 42 hand points. A mark bid of N marks requires the bidding team to take all 42 points of the hand; falling even one point short is a set.

## Communication

Partners may not communicate hand information during play. Decisions are based only on public information (the bid, the trump declaration, the tricks already played, the lead, and the player's own hand).

## Facts this primer commits to

- The domino set has exactly 28 dominoes, drawn from the double-six set.
- Four players play in two fixed partnerships; partners sit across the table.
- Each player is dealt exactly 7 dominoes; no dominoes remain undealt.
- There are exactly 7 tricks per hand.
- A hand is worth exactly 42 points: 35 count + 7 trick points.
- The five count dominoes are 5-5, 6-4, 5-0, 4-1, and 3-2.
- 5-5 is worth 10 count points.
- 6-4 is worth 10 count points.
- 5-0 is worth 5 count points.
- 4-1 is worth 5 count points.
- 3-2 is worth 5 count points.
- All 23 other dominoes are worth 0 count points.
- Each trick is worth exactly 1 trick point.
- Trump declarations reduce to: one of 7 pip suits, doubles-as-trump, or no-trump.
- When fives are trump, 5-5 is the highest trump.
- When a pip suit S is trump, every domino containing pip S is trump and the S-S double is the highest trump.
- When doubles are trump, the trump suit consists of exactly the 7 doubles, ranked 6-6 high through 0-0 low.
- When doubles are trump, a double is not a member of its pip suit.
- When a pip suit is trump, the 6-4 is trump if and only if that pip suit is sixes or fours.
- The led suit of a lead is trump if the led domino is a trump; otherwise it is the higher pip of the led domino.
- With fours as trump, leading 4-2 leads trump.
- With twos as trump, leading 5-3 leads the suit of fives.
- A player must follow the led suit if and only if they hold at least one domino of that suit.
- A player who cannot follow the led suit may play any domino, including a trump.
- If any trumps are played to a trick, the highest trump played wins.
- If no trumps are played to a trick, the highest domino of the led suit wins.
- Under no-trump, no domino can beat a domino of the led suit from another suit.
- The winner of each trick leads the next trick.
- The bidder's team makes the bid if and only if they take at least as many of the 42 hand points as the bid value (for point bids 30-41), or all 42 points (for mark bids).
- A mark bid requires sweeping all 42 points of the hand.
- 1 mark equals 42 points.
