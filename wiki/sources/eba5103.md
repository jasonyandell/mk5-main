## Commit message

> feat(gus/eval): detect-and-route inference wrapper (end-to-end validation)
>
> Router implements three fallback policies — oracle argmax, PIMC via
> student Q_head averaged across K=50 sampled worlds, and next-best-adapter.
> Loads the student-feature blunder detector (trained on 30% of train
> chunks 0-299) and flags decisions above configurable thresholds.
>
> Empirical reality check on 560 held-out decisions (baseline 1.39 regret):
>
>   flag%  oracle   pimc-q-k50   next-best (v1_full_1000g)
>   5%     1.15     1.39         1.48
>   20%    0.56     1.47 (!)     1.55 (!)
>   25%    0.49     1.48         1.69
>
> Findings:
>   1. Oracle routing works as projected — 0.49 regret at 25% flag.
>   2. PIMC-Q-K50 HURTS: fixes 7 blunders but introduces 6 new ones
>      on non-blunder decisions the detector incorrectly flags.
>      Q_head trained on one random world per forward is too noisy.
>   3. Next-best-adapter is worst — smaller/weaker adapters also
>      wrong on the sharp decisions where primary needs help.
>
> Practical implication: detect-and-route needs oracle calls to ship.
> Without oracle budget, Q_head multi-world variance regularization is
> the prerequisite experiment — already flagged in PRACTICALITIES #13.
>
> Router benefit concentrates on mid-game tricks (dec 0-12, especially
> 4, 8, 10) where primary regret is 2-6 Q-pt. End-game decisions
> (24-27) are correctly never flagged.
