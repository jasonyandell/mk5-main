## Commit message

> eval(gus): regret-based eval — E[Q] lost vs oracle's best legal
>
> For each held-out decision: regret = oracle_best_eq - student_chosen_eq.
> Better signal of play quality than bot-match because 42 is full of
> near-tie positions where multiple actions have E[Q] within 0.1 of each
> other — the student picking the oracle's 2nd choice is not a miss.
>
> Empirical (1000g adapters, 560 held-out decisions):
>   v1_full_1000g:       bot 65.4%, regret 2.16 Q-pt, near-ties 73.4%
>   v2_voids_1000g:      bot 63.6%, regret 2.22 Q-pt, near-ties 72.0%
>
> 2.16 Q-points of mean regret on a [-42, +42] scale = ~2.5% of Q range.
> That's the honest read on this student's play quality: the 35%
> "bot-mismatches" are overwhelmingly near-tie alternative choices, not
> strategic blunders.
>
> Hardest decisions (by regret):
>   dec 4:  8.64 regret, 25% bot-match
>   dec 8:  4.12 regret, 30% bot-match
>   dec 11: 4.87 regret, 75% bot-match  (near-ties inflate bot-match here)
>
> End-game (dec 24-27): 0 regret, 100% match. Perfect.

Introduces regret as the primary eval metric and reframes 35% "bot-mismatches" as
overwhelmingly near-tie alternative choices.
