## Premise

> Can a small model play 42 through **tool orchestration** — asking the engine for facts, asking Zeb for beliefs, reasoning between calls — instead of memorizing the game in its weights?

([[burl]], `burl/OVERVIEW.md` @ 8d26e0d)

LEM hit a 55/100 bot-match ceiling that gradient reallocation and capacity scaling didn't move. Burl tries a different shape: tools replace memorization. The engine is the authority on rules; [[zeb]] is the authority on hidden-state beliefs; Burl's job is reasoning with what the tools return and committing to a play.

## LEM vs Burl — not a successor, a sibling

| | LEM | Burl |
|---|---|---|
| Base model | Qwen 3 1.7B | Gemma 4 E2B (agentic-strong) |
| Training data | Flashcards: `(state, Q) → A` | Trajectories: tool calls + play |
| Objective | Pattern-match comprehension | Tool-mediated play |
| Eval | Comprehension accuracy, rationalization | Legal rate, retry count, bot-match |
| Product slot | "Explain this position" mode | "Play against AI" mode |
| Lineage | Fresh from Qwen 3 | Fresh from Gemma 4 (no LEM lineage) |
| Directory | `lem/` | `burl/` |

Shared infrastructure (owned by neither): `forge/` — engine, E[Q] framework, solver, [[zeb]], visualizer.

## Why Gemma 4 E2B for Burl

Comprehension matters less for Burl (tools provide facts); training volume is smaller (trajectories, not tens of thousands of flashcards); Gemma 4 was designed for agentic function-calling with native tool-use training and thinking mode actively used during tool loops; agentic benchmarks favor Gemma 4 at the 2B scale. The architectural issues that hurt LEM training throughput (PLE, KV-sharing, no FA2) have lower priority on smaller trajectory corpora. Provisional — revisit if prototype says otherwise.

## Vocabulary (precise definitions)

> | Term | What it is | Where it lives |
> |---|---|---|
> | **Perfect-information solver** | Takes a fully-specified deal + declaration, enumerates reachable states, solves by backward induction / minimax. Deterministic. | `forge/oracle/` |
> | **E[Q] framework** | Uses the solver as a subroutine. For an imperfect-info position, enumerates plausible hidden-hand realizations, evaluates each via the solver, averages weighted by prior. | `forge/eq/` |
> | **E[Q] bot** | The `argmax(E[Q])` player. Picks the legal move with highest expected value. This is the comparison target in K1 grading. | Throughout game + narration code |
> | **Zeb** | Learned belief model (3.3M-param transformer). Predicts `P(opponent ∈ {L, partner, R} | visible state)` for each of 28 dominoes. 72% top-1 accuracy. | `forge/zeb/` |
> | **Engine** | The TypeScript game engine — rules, state transitions, legality, visible information. | `src/core/` |

Do NOT conflate the solver with the E[Q] framework. The solver sees everything and is deterministic. The E[Q] framework runs the solver over a distribution of hidden states and averages. Burl is graded against the E[Q] framework; it never calls the solver directly.

## Architecture

Three components at inference time: [[burl]] (Gemma 4 E2B reasoning + orchestration), [[engine]] (epistemic facts), [[zeb]] (learned beliefs). Composed via the `conditional_outcome` higher-order tool: Zeb gives P(assumption | visible); E[Q] framework evaluates outcome conditional on assumption; result returned to Burl.

The E[Q] framework and perfect-info solver live behind the inference-time boundary — used for training-side grading (K1) and dev-time work only. Burl never calls them directly.

## Tool surface

**Allowed** (epistemic — facts about current state): `is_legal`, `is_trump`, `unseen`, `void_audit`, `trump_declared`, `get_belief`, `posterior`, `conditional_outcome`.

**Forbidden** (evaluative — distillation short-circuits): `get_eq(dom)`, `best_move()`, `simulate_plan(actions)`. Rule: tools answer "**what IS the state?**", never "**what SHOULD you do?**".

## First experimental moves

1. **Move 1** — Wrap `forge/zeb/` as `burl/tools/zeb.py`. Pure software, no ML. Prereq for everything.
2. **Move 2** — Zeb calibration eval (Brier score, reliability diagram). Cheap; uses existing checkpoint.
3. **Move 3** — Prototype with base Gemma 4 + tool harness, zero training (20–50 decisions). Cheapest way to assess whether the Gemma 4 base is agentic enough.
4. **Move 4** — Training corpus design based on Move 3 failure modes.
5. **Move 5** — First SFT run + STaR iteration loop.
