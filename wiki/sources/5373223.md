## Commit message

> feat(gus/eval): student-feature blunder detector (deployable)
>
> Follow-up to the oracle-feature detector (f90682c) — retrains using
> only features derivable at inference from the student's own outputs,
> not from the oracle's E[Q] vector. This is what actually ships.
>
> Feature set (28 dims, all student-derivable):
> - Policy: pi_peak, pi_entropy, pi_argmax_margin (softmax over legal)
> - V_head: scalar
> - Q_head across K=20 sampled worlds per decision: per-action mean,
>   std, min, max; legal-action spread and std of per-action means
> - Consistency gaps: V_head vs policy-expected-Q, V_head vs Q_mean_chosen
> - Meta: decision_idx, trick_num, trick_pos, player_rel, legal count,
>   declaration one-hot, voids count
> - Belief: max prob, entropy, high-confidence count
>
> Results (same train/test split as v1):
>   v1 (oracle features):    ROC-AUC 0.926, PR-AUC 0.29
>   v2 (student features):   ROC-AUC 0.839, PR-AUC 0.15
>
> Business case at 20% flag rate with oracle-argmax replacement:
>   baseline regret 1.13 → 0.49 (57% reduction)
>
> Top feature: pi_peak (0.19) — the student's own policy confidence.
> Intuitive: "when π_me is uncertain, trigger fallback."
>
> Key finding: student's Q_head spread across worlds underperforms as
> a blunder proxy compared to oracle's E[Q] spread. Q_head trained on
> one world per forward pass — its spread signal is noisy. Two natural
> upgrades: multi-world variance regularization during Q_head training
> (affects train_v2_voids.py), or K=50+ worlds at inference (cheap).
>
> Detect-and-route is deployable. ~0.5 Q-pt regret is achievable from
> inference-only features — the practical floor for vanilla distillation.

## What this commit establishes

Deployable detector at AUC 0.839 using only student outputs. Top feature `pi_peak`
means "when the policy is uncertain, call for help." Q_head spread is a weak blunder
proxy — requires multi-world variance regularization or larger K to close the gap vs
oracle spread.
