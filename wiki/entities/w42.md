## Summary

w42 is the Winning 42 workstream. **Since 2026-10-14 the book is the authority
and our measurement is the suspect** ([[book-authority]]). Where Zeb and the book
part, the finding is what our arena cannot yet see. The book's implicit
assumptions, the lessons that depend on them, the measurements that would
restore them, and the ledger to completion are in [[book-assumptions]]. The
oracle-era campaign (2026-05) treated the book as a source of hypotheses, not
ground truth. That stance is retired; its pages are history.

The workstream turns lessons like "bid only enough," "donate count safely,"
"double ahead of your off," "create a pounce window," and "preserve the 84
stopper" into things the project can encode, seat, measure, and explain. The
goal is the book covered in full, every lesson stated or implicit.

For the oracle era, [[w42-book-validation]] is the curated path through its
evidence pile and [[w42-book-claim-synthesis-and-ai-directions]] its synthesis.
The live route is [[book-authority]] → [[book-assumptions]]. This page is only
the frontier hub.

## Boundary

[[gus]] remains the oracle-distillation sibling: w42 may borrow Gus corpus
formats, public-state encodings, and strategy-probe lessons, but it does not
turn Gus into a mixed research workbench. [[burl]] remains the tool-using
agent: w42 may inspect Burl traces when the question is about reasoning or
explanation, but book claims do not become Burl rules without an explicit
later decision. The standing boundary decision is [[w42-promote-or-retire]]:
keep w42 as research, promote durable code/reports into `w42/`, and do not
promote research models into Gus or Burl yet.

w42 owns the charter, claim ledger, wiki pages, report conventions, detector
definitions, cheap supervised probes, and the workstream's
promotion/retirement decisions. It borrows [[forge]] E[Q] labels and
perfect-info oracle caveats, [[gus]] public-state corpora,
[[forge-analysis]] report discipline, and
[[winning42-strategy-measurement]]'s chapter inventory. It does not touch Gus
core training defaults, forge oracle semantics, Burl harness behavior, book
claims as hard-coded play rules, or private information unavailable from
legal public game state.

## Current Frontier

The phase-4 closure sweep gave all 64 book-claim ledger rows evidence or a
bounded blocker; "closed" meant an owner or a blocker, not a test.
[[w42-phase4-final-claim-audit]] is the closure baseline;
[[w42-book-claim-synthesis-and-ai-directions]] is the campaign's synthesis.

[[w42-book-validation-campaign]] is finished: nothing has run since Wave 5
(2026-06-13). **It established no strategic claim from the book.** Its 24
`supported` rows are 20 arithmetic, rule-fixture or scoring-definition rows, two
near-tautological negative controls (the cost of dumping count equals the
count), bid-only-enough (a tautology: at one mark a higher bid can only lower
P(make)), and pounce-before-certainty (null on its literal label). Every
tactical number is in points, labelled by a learned Q network (qMAE 0.94) on a
bid-30, oracle-played, 100-deal corpus later found 27–67% malformed, with no
chance baseline beside any agreement rate (random legal play matched the
oracle's pick 57% of the time). The setter-side probe verdicts (void creation,
both pounces) are withdrawn pending a sign check (#122); the ledger CSV is
hand-edited and disagrees with its own summaries (#125).
[[w42-lens-v1-utility-head-to-head]]'s EV-beats-p_make result survives in
marks; the rest of its ordering does not. The campaign's diagnosis of itself
was wrong: a perfect-information evaluator plans perfectly within each world;
its defect is strategy fusion ([[the-wall]]).

[[book-strategy-player]] was the promoted route (2026-05-04) for testing
those multi-step plans; it was never built — superseded before build by the
auction-first [[jud]] redirection ([[champion-design-review]],
2026-06-09). [[w42-champion-teaching-battery]] later answered "does the
champion agree with the book?" directly against champion self-play
trajectories, bypassing BSP entirely.

The current frontier for "what is the best 42 player" is [[jud]] v1
([[w42-jud-v1]], graded 2026-07-06): the value-native bidder
`margin:wp`(head_8)+`lens:ev` is the best player the project knows how to
build, reconfirmed the same day by the Zeb-protocol measurement at `afd4802`
(eq n=10 still undefeated at pure play against every learned challenger since
Zeb; the `margin:wp` bidding crown holds against the live, non-distilled Gus
sim bidder too, +0.59 [-0.19,+1.39], 64 games). See [[jud]] for the player and
[[champion-ladder]] for the full ladder.

**2026-10-12: the book read with the honest tools.** [[book-study-2026-10-12]] re-asked
the book's claims against Zeb ([[scenario-player]]) instead of the oracle: 21 rule gates
priced on the 250,000-deal pour, the bid book against Zeb's forecast, the odds appendix by
exact count (three figures corrected), and the conventions graded as joint deviations in
S/R/SR arena arms (all null or negative). On the book's 18 rules as the book means them
([[silas]]; 17 fire at bid 30), Zeb's pick is the book's move at 56% of the positions each
rule governs against a 43% random floor (chance-corrected 0.24); it sides with 3 rules,
agrees weakly with 9, shows no relation to 2, sits between the bands on 3 and prices against
none. The 21 gates first priced (52% against 37%;
E[Q] 56%) were the book's rule for only 3 of them over the whole pour. The two rules first
priced against (the dead double, the trump set) fired where a condition the book states
fails; with it they show no relation. The bid book's 25 of 30 beats
"always bid" by two hands, and Zeb bids 84 on none of the book's six 84 hands. No reason
from the book was adopted: the reason report (#124) explains Zeb's tile where it deviates,
not the book's. Read under [[book-authority]]: these prices measure distance from Zeb's plan in
Zeb's own self-play worlds, not the book's value at its table.

**2026-10-13/14: the book seated, investigated, and re-framed.**
- *The head-to-head* ([[book-head-to-head-2026-10-13]]): the 18 rules as one player against
  deployed Zeb score −1.88 [−2.09, −1.66] marks/game against a 0 baseline. The arena has no
  cross-hand modeling, no partner reading and no fallible opponents.
- *The investigation* ([[book-investigation-2026-10-14]]): 0.47 of that was our encoding
  (repaired: −1.41 [−1.63, −1.18]). Restoring the book's own game, points to 250, uncovered its
  first measured edge: as Zeb's near-tie break it wins 53.2% [51.1, 55.2] of games, replicated.
- *A within-hand reader by the book* ([[book-reads-by-the-book-2026-10-14]]): null for the book
  player (−0.04 [−0.27, +0.19]). Plain Zeb beats that reader by +0.50.
- *Book partners reading each other by the book* ([[book-partners-read-2026-10-14]], #150): the
  receiving half of A2 seated. +0.10 [−0.06, +0.26] marks/game against deployed Zeb 0.3.0, null at
  this power, every sign positive. The book's receivers live in rule guards, which cannot yet read.
- *Soft play-reading* ([[reads-plays-soft-2026-10-14]]), a crude within-hand model of the other
  players, made Zeb stronger by +0.37 [+0.24, +0.51] marks/game, replicated. Modeling others
  pays even in our arena.
- *The Book Authority* ([[book-authority]], [[book-assumptions]]): the stance, its vocabulary,
  the inventory of eleven implicit assumptions, and the completion ledger. The next experiment
  is the regulars' table (#149): does an opponent who models you across hands make Zeb's
  determinism cost, and the book's variation pay?

The live w42 workstream (from 2026-10-12) is a formal study of the book: catalog
the reasoning of every worked example and lesson, from the raw page images, and
for each ask two separate questions — can Zeb (or an exact enumerator, where the
position allows) *explain* the book's move, and does it *agree*. Its first
pieces are the book's examples as positions (#123) and Zeb's reason report
(#124); its rules are written in [[silas]]. [[w42-book-second-pass]] (2026-07-07) remains the best inventory of the
book's information theory (the auction decoder, signaling conventions,
match-score-conditioned bidding), none of which any study has tested.

Two detector-hygiene bugs remain important:

- `ch05_reckless_count` overfires when it cannot distinguish reckless count into
  bidder control from the broader regime.
- `ch05_setter_pressure_regime` is a regime feature, not an action endorsement.

## Evaluation Principles

- Separate "can this claim be measured?" from "does this feature improve a
  model?" — detectors, tables, and reports come before model inputs.
- Scalar E[Q] is not always enough: branch-shaped states need distribution
  evidence (threshold mass, quantiles, tail risk, hidden-holder impact).
- Public-state safety: offline reports may use sampled hidden worlds, but live
  features come only from legal public state or learned beliefs.
- Granularity: single-decision probes are the cheap first pass, but
  plan-shaped claims need plan-shaped tests.

## What our model leaves out

Every arena measurement of the book so far seats a table the book was not written for
([[book-assumptions]]). The table has opponents who never model you across hands (A1), a
partner who never reads the conventions (A2), and opponents who never lose track (A3). It
leaves the auction unread (A4), has a field with no bidding styles (A6), and declares only
pip trumps (A10). The book's edge is presumed to live in these until measurements that hold
them say otherwise: #149, #151, #152, #154, #155; A2's receiving half was seated by #150
([[book-partners-read-2026-10-14]], null) and waits on a guard that reads (#128).
