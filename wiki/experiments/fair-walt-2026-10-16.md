## Summary

Issue #181, under the FAIR CLOCKS rule (CLAUDE.md, Jason, 2026-10-16: "all players in the arena should
be made fair not wall"). Production Walt (`walt-plunge-26043bf9`) was the one tournament player that
thought on a wall clock. The pinned `walt-player.wasm` reads time only through the host import
`now_us`, so a new participant, `walt-plunge-26043bf9-fair`, seats the same wasm, book and manifest
(the same sha256s) behind a host whose `now_us` is a counter. This page pre-registers how the counter's
step is chosen, then reports it ([[tournament]]).

## Pre-registered (written before any timing)

**Panel.** `python -m tournament.fair_walt_panel build` ([[tournament]] code,
`tournament/fair_walt_panel.py`): 24 auction positions (Walt's live survey: four rounds of nine
pricing jobs, 4/12/40/160 worlds), 8 opening leads by the bidder (160 worlds, 20 s) and 16 other
plays spread over the hand (40 worlds, partner review, 14 s), recorded from seeded games of the
arena's heuristic bidder and uniform-legal play (base seeds 181000 and up). None of the 24 auction
hands is in the frozen book, so each is a live survey.

**Measurement.** `node tournament/seat/calibrate-fair-walt.mjs reads`: every panel call on the
pinned wasm under the real clock, one call at a time, counting `now_us` reads and the seconds the
call took, one pass, inside `scripts/cpu-slot --exclusive`. R is the pooled rate, total reads over
total seconds.

**Rule.** The step is `clock_step_us = 1e6 / R`, rounded to three decimals: one virtual second is
the work production does in a real second on this machine, so 14 s, 20 s and 500 ms mean the work
production reaches in 14 s, 20 s and 500 ms. It is fixed by this rule alone; the paired check
below cannot move it.

**Match check (thinking).** The same panel through `host.mjs` under the production config and the
fair config, same quiet machine, one after the other: mean wall seconds per request and the share
of requests with the same reply. Expected: the same replies wherever no deadline binds, which on a
quiet machine is nearly every request (a 160-world opening lead takes about half a second against
a 20 s deadline).

**Paired check.** Fair against production in duplicate pairs (the tournament's own faceoff, marks to
7), run inside `scripts/cpu-slot --exclusive`. Baseline 50%. The interval is the ladder's
(`paired_margin`, 95%). If the two were bit-identical every pair would split 1-1 and one version
would be enough; if not, the measure is how often the games differ and by how much.

## Results

All on this Mac (18 cores), each inside `scripts/cpu-slot --exclusive`, 2026-10-16, mk5 branch
`lead/fair-walt`. Panel sha256 `2160a47f…` (24 + 8 + 16 requests).

**Calibration.** Production's wasm made 118,671,371 `now_us` reads in 99.58 s over the panel's 48
requests: **R = 1,191,753 reads per second**, so **`clock_step_us = 0.839`** (1e6 / R = 0.8391). By
kind: auction surveys 1,197,598 reads/s (24 requests, 96.8 s, up to 6.9 M reads and 5.6 s a survey
run one job at a time), opening leads 981,368 (8, 0.33 s each, at most 0.51 s), other plays
1,157,379 (16, at most 0.034 s). A read is about one per 256 nodes, so it counts work, and the rate
differs by about 20% across kinds, not by orders of magnitude.

**What binds.** Nothing in the panel comes within a factor of three of a deadline: the longest
survey is 5.6 s against 20 s, the longest opening lead 0.5 s against 20 s, the longest other play
34 ms against 14 s. On a quiet Mac production's clock does not decide a move. It would decide
moves on a slower device (a phone) or a loaded machine, which is exactly the part that was
unfair; the step above is the Mac's, so on a slower device the fair Walt thinks the same work
and takes longer.

**Match check (thinking), same panel through `host.mjs`.** 48 of 48 replies identical (24 bids,
24 plays). Mean wall seconds a request: bids 1.30 production, 1.42 fair; plays 0.119 and 0.119.
The fair survey is about 9% slower in wall on one run each (the survey runs a round of jobs at
once and replays the 4-worker schedule afterwards; one run each cannot separate that from noise).

**Bit-identity, played out (added after the paired check, not pre-registered).** Same deals, same game keys (so the same seeds), both teams the same
Walt: production, production again, and the fair Walt, 6 games (3 duplicate pairs, 69 hands).
Every hand (bids, declarations, every play) is **identical in 6 of 6 games across all three
runs**, production against its own rerun included. On a quiet machine, where no deadline binds,
the fair Walt *is* production Walt, move for move; they differ only where production's clock would
have mattered. (`scratch/fair/bitcheck.py`, sha256 `c09f771b…`.)

**Paired check, fair vs production, 12 duplicate pairs (24 games), marks to 7.** The fair Walt
won **13 of 24 games (54%, Wilson 95% [35%, 72%]; baseline 50%)** and **+0.29 marks/game [−0.77,
+1.35]** (the ladder's cluster-robust interval, `paired_margin`). Seven of the 12 pairs split 1–1.
It is a tie within what 12 pairs can see; the interval is about ±1 mark, so it cannot show the two
within a few tenths of a mark. The pairs cannot be bit-compared: a game's seed is its key, which
carries the seating, so the two games of a pair never replay one another even for identical
players. The bit-identity run above is the sharp measure; this one says the games a fair seat
plays win and lose like production's. Record (fair first): a 144–137 marks edge. Both sides spent
the same CPU, 3107 s and 3166 s (instructions 3.34e13 and 3.43e13). (`scratch/fair/paired.py`,
sha256 `700e6ed8…`; the faceoff record `noisy_timed` False, run under `--exclusive`.)

**Reading.** Jason's test was that if they were bit-identical one version would be enough. On a
quiet machine they are, in every panel request and every game played. The fair Walt is not a
second player; it is production Walt with the machine taken out of the loop, and the new
participant id exists because participants are immutable and production's record says
`"clock": "real"`.

**Limits.** One machine, one device speed, one run of each measurement; the panel is random-play
positions (so the 40-world plays are lighter than a Walt-vs-Walt middle game); a step calibrated
on this Mac makes the budget bind later than a phone's would. The survey's rerun rule is
conservative by two clock steps, and its determinism was checked by eight concurrent runs of a
deadline-binding step (20 µs a read) giving one survey hash.
