## Summary

Jason, 2026-10-13: "I think we will see overlap. or places to hedge. or monte hall
situations… by things that happen in different lumps. eg the 5-5 is gonna play a role all
over… money on the table… might be informative if it is short to test."

At every live position of 10,000 deals of the Zeb pour, for every tile T the mover cannot
see, the worlds are split into three **lumps** by who holds T, and two questions are asked of
Zeb's own per-move, per-world margins ([[metasplat-2026-10-13]]): is one move best in every
lump (**robust**) or do the lumps disagree (**hedge**); and what would knowing T's seat be
worth before this move (**VOI**, value of information). No perfect-information value is used anywhere: every value is
the plan's, with the root move varied and the continuation fixed.

**What it shows.** Beyond the sampling bar, 96.5% of (position, tile) pairs are robust on the
make. On average the money is small: knowing one tile is worth 0.49 points of make probability (pp)
against 0.32 from an arbitrary split of the same sizes. **The 5-5 is the tile most worth knowing**, ahead of the 6-4,
4-4, 6-6 and 6-5: 0.39 pp of make beyond the null and 0.26 points of margin. Where the 5-5 is not trump, 2.3% of its rows
carry 5 pp or more, against 0.17% for a meaningless split. The money peaks at tricks 4–5. It sits
more than its share in the lump where the mover's **partner** holds the tile: 43% of the 5-5's
VOI against 33% of the mass. Restricted choice, the bridge Monty Hall, exists in 42 exactly when
a seat's choice between tiles is read as a uniform pick. Seeing the left setter follow with the 5-0 moves
"he holds the 5-5" from 6/19 to 615/2708 under the uniform likelihood, and leaves it at
6/19 under the consistent one.

## Fixed before counting

Fixed in `scenario/lumps_voi.py` and committed (`798ec7ad`) before the first count. Nothing below
moved after the counts; three descriptive additions made after them are marked.

- **Population.** Every planned position of deals 0–9,999 of the pour's chunk s100001 not
  decided by the banked score (the metasplat's rule): 133,793 positions. One row per tile hidden
  from the mover (not played, not in his hand): 1,771,439 rows. A played or held tile has one
  lump and nothing to sell, so it has no row.
- **Lumps.** The worlds where T is with the mover's left, his partner, his right.
- **Values,** the mover's (the metasplat's units): p_L(a) the share of lump L's worlds in which
  move a succeeds (made for the bidders, set for the setters), μ_L(a) its mean signed margin in
  points. Best "by make" is the largest p, then μ, then the lowest tile; "by margin" the largest μ, then p.
- **Minimum mass.** A lump is non-trivial at 10% of the cell's worlds or more. A row is *split* when
  two lumps are non-trivial (98.6% of rows). Otherwise T's seat is nearly known and the row is not classified.
- **Hedge or robust,** per split row. *Robust:* some move is within the lump's bar of the
  lump's best in every non-trivial lump. *Hedge:* no move is. The bar is 2 paired standard errors
  of the difference over the lump's worlds, and zero in an enumerated cell (under 512 worlds,
  the whole fiber). *Strict:* the same best move in every non-trivial lump, with no bar.
- **VOI(T)** = Σ_L mass_L · max_a v_L(a) − max_a v(a), for p and for μ. By the information
  inequality (an average of maxima is at least the maximum of the average) it is ≥ 0. It is an
  **upper side**: the gain of a mover told T's seat before this one move, with Zeb's
  continuation unchanged. Zeb cannot collect it, and on a sample the maximum within each lump inflates it.
- **The noise bar.** Every row's VOI is computed again with its lump labels shuffled over the
  cell's worlds, keeping the sizes. This *null* is what an arbitrary split earns from the maximum over moves
  alone. *Excess* is VOI less null. Means carry 95% intervals clustered by deal.

## Money on the table

The raw largest VOI are endgame guesses in cells of 2–12 worlds. The partner holds the
4-3 or the bidder does, and the right play is a coin. These positions are chosen after
seeing that, by the largest excess with every non-trivial lump at least 32 worlds, one per
deal (`top.json`). The mover's view; p is the make probability (the set's, for a setter).

1. **Cover or duck, by where the 6-4 sits** (deal 4120, fours, trick 2). The partner leads
   the trump 4-2; the right setter follows with the 4-1, and the bidder holds the 4-4, 5-4, 4-3, 4-0 with
   only the 6-4 out. If the left setter, still to play, holds it, cover with the 4-4 (p 0.99). If
   the right setter holds it, he has already played, so win with the 5-4 and keep the 4-4 to
   catch the 6-4 later (p 0.97 against the 4-4's 0.26). Zeb covers with the 4-4 every time and
   gives up 0.71 in the third of the worlds where the right setter holds it: **24 pp on the table**.
2. **Duck, unless the 4-4 is in front** (deal 1733, fours, trick 4). The bidder's partner
   leads the 5-4. The right setter holds the 6-4 and 4-2. If the bidder, behind him, holds the 4-4,
   duck with the 4-2 (p 0.99); so too if his own partner holds it. If the leader holds it, the 6-4
   wins the trick and its 10 (p 1.0 against the duck's 0.12). Zeb ducks: **25 pp**, all of it in
   the 28% where the leader holds the 4-4. Would a leader holding the 4-4 have led the 5-4? Reading that
   lead as evidence is the next section's question.
3. **The 5-5 decides the bidder's discard** (deal 3986, fours, trick 5). The right setter
   leads the 2-0 and the bidder holds the 4-1, 5-0, 5-4. If the partner holds the 5-5, trump
   with the 4-1 (p 0.96). If a setter holds it, throw the 5-0 (p 0.59 or 0.48). Zeb throws
   the 5-0 and gives up 0.61 in the 38% where the partner holds it: **23 pp**.
4. **The bidder's opening lead depends on who holds the 5-5** (deal 6153, treys, the
   opening). Holding the 3-0, 3-1, 3-3, 4-3, 4-4, 5-0 and 6-3: if the partner holds the 5-5, lead the
   3-3 (p 0.95). If the left setter does, the 3-0 (0.52). If the right setter, the 4-3 (0.41). Zeb leads the
   3-0 and gives up 0.20 in the partner's lump and 0.04 in the right's: **7.8 pp**, the
   largest 5-5 opening in the sample (`five_five_opening`). At the opening even the largest is small.
5. **Where the 6-6 sits decides the lead** (deal 6233, treys, trick 5). The bidder holds the 4-3, 5-3, 6-4. If
   the left setter holds the 6-6, lead the 6-4 (p 0.79 against Zeb's 5-3 at 0.11). If the
   partner does, the 4-3 (1.0). Zeb leads the 5-3: **24 pp**.

In 3 and 4, and in three of the 5-5's own top five (`five_five`), the money is in the lump
where the mover's partner holds the tile. Zeb plays the move that is right when an opponent holds it,
because the opponents' two lumps carry two-thirds of the mass. In 1, 2 and 5 the money is in one
opponent's lump: a position guess, cover or duck by which opponent sits behind.

## The Monty Hall angle: restricted choice

In bridge, *restricted choice* says that a defender who plays one of two equivalent cards
was more likely dealt the singleton. With both he would have had a choice, and he plays
each half the time. In 42 the analogue is exact once a seat's choice is modeled. [[silas]]
reads plays as evidence (`silas query … --read SEAT=POLICY --likelihood consistent|uniform`; its README §
The record as evidence). Under the **consistent** likelihood a world is kept when the policy
allows the play. Under the **uniform** likelihood it weighs 1/|allowed set|, so the size of the
choice becomes evidence.

**A constructed example** (`restricted-choice/` in the Data). Sixes trump, bid 30. The bidder leads the 5-4 and
the left setter follows with the 5-0. From the partner's seat, before he plays, the question is
whether the left setter holds the 5-5. The left setter is read under a policy with no rules, which allows every legal
tile: following fives, every five he holds.

| belief | P(left setter holds the 5-5) | P(the 5-0 was his only five) |
|---|---|---|
| unread (the uniform cell) | 6/19 = 31.6% | 715/3876 = 18.4% |
| read, consistent | 6/19 = 31.6% | 715/3876 = 18.4% |
| read, uniform | **615/2708 = 22.7%** | **715/2031 = 35.2%** |

The 615/2708 is Silas's exact count (`exact_given(S1 plays uniformly within
any-legal-tile)`), and the same fraction comes from summing over his possible hands by hand:
his other 6 tiles from 19, 4 of them fives, each hand weighted 1/(1 + his other fives).

**In plain words.** A follower with the 5-0 alone had to play it. A follower with the 5-0 and the
5-5 (or any other five) would have shown the 5-0 only some of the time. So seeing the 5-0 makes
"no other five" nearly twice as likely (18% to 35%), and the 5-5 behind it less likely (32% to
23%). The consistent reading only asks whether the play was allowed, and the 5-0 was allowed in
every hand, so it learns nothing.

**What it is not.** The example's follower picks at random. A real setter holding the 5-5 wins the
5-4 with it, and then the 5-0 rules the 5-5 out under either likelihood: that is inference, not
restricted choice. Restricted choice needs two tiles a policy treats as equal, such as two low
non-count followers or two counts a donation rule allows alike.

**Where it touches Zeb.** The pour's worlds are uniform over the cell: no play is read as
evidence. Every lump mass above is therefore the consistent-likelihood mass, and a restricted-choice
shift is information Zeb leaves unused. Position 2 above shows the shape. Under a
lead-the-top-trump policy, the leader's 5-4 says he lacks the 4-4, and that empties the very
lump the money sits in.

**Next step (not done).** Read one seat of a real pour position under a book policy whose
allowed set holds two equivalent tiles (`low-from-a-pair`, `donate-when-safe`). Reweight that
position's 512 worlds by the uniform likelihood, and recompute the lump masses and the VOI. If
the partner's lump shrinks where the leader had a choice, restricted choice prices.

**Priced in play (2026-10-14):** [[reads-plays-2026-10-13]] reads every other seat's plays
by the field net's set under both likelihoods and plays the reader against plain Zeb.
Restricted choice is +0.10 [−0.15, +0.36] marks/game, a null. The reading's changed moves
carry this page's tiles (the 5-5 and 6-4 first, then the 4-4 and the 5-counts) and peak at
tricks 4–5, with a slight lean to the partner's lump. Softened so the posterior never empties
([[reads-plays-soft-2026-10-14]]), the reading pays (+0.23 marks/game where the model is true,
+0.3 against Zeb at first, +0.12 [−0.02, +0.27] (consistent) and +0.23 (uniform) in the
registered replication), but restricted choice is still null even at a table that plays the uniform
model: +0.09 [−0.08, +0.26].

## Caveats

- VOI is an upper side twice over. It is the information inequality's gap, and on a sample the
  maximum within each lump inflates it. The null measures the second inflation and the excess
  removes it on average, but not row by row.
- One continuation: the root move changes and Zeb's plan plays on unchanged. A mover who knew T
  would also continue differently, so the continuation's own value of information is not in these numbers.
- The plan's own error (another seed, a stronger rung) is not in the bar
  ([[book-study-2026-10-12]] § The face-off: 0.005–0.016 pmake per decision).
- Rows overlap: one position contributes one row per hidden tile, and the tiles are correlated.
  Intervals cluster by deal.
- The producing player is the pour's (`k0,n512`, `make`, bid 30, seat 0 bids, `field_make_d5000_v2`,
  before #110 and #112), not the deployed Zeb; 10,000 of the chunk's 50,000 deals.
- Under `make`, margins past a decided contract are the tape's, not the plan's (#107), so VOI_μ there
  is a fact about the continuation.
