## Summary

This report validates the deterministic part of Chapter 10's scoring-objective
drift claims for [[w42]]. It covers point scoring, mark scoring, 84/126/168 mark
multipliers, tournament-style hand-win objectives, and synthetic objective
transforms.

Epistemic status: confirmed result for scoring algebra and synthetic objective
transforms; not a [[forge]] E[Q] rollout, [[gus]]/w42 model result, [[burl]]
trace review, human-game study, or tournament bracket simulation.

Headline: Chapter 10's main accounting claims are supported in the tested
deterministic scope. Point scoring preserves captured-point and set-severity
information; marks collapse ordinary bids to a one-mark make/set threshold;
84/126/168 scale to two/three/four marks; and tournament hand-win objectives
erase even the mark multiplier when only the hand result matters. The policy
claim that point scoring is a better skill signal remains underpowered until a
policy population or oracle arena is run.

## Key Question

How do point scoring, mark scoring, 84 multipliers, tournament objectives, and
simple objective transforms change the right scoring target before any model or
oracle rollout is used?

## Findings

| finding | evidence | metric | status |
|---|---|---|---|
| Marks change ordinary bids from point-rich outcomes to a one-mark make/set threshold. | `terminal_objective_transform.csv`; `summary.json` | 516 ordinary terminal rows; 246 made rows erase defender partial points | supported |
| Ordinary failed bids have many point severities but the same mark result. | `summary.json` | 41 distinct ordinary set penalties, from 43 to 83 points, compressed to one mark | supported |
| Mark terminal states can occur before the seventh trick. | `early_mark_terminal_states.csv`; `summary.json` | 14,623 arithmetic terminal rows; max 6 tricks potentially saved | supported |
| 84/126/168 are multiplier objectives, not ordinary point-margin objectives. | `objective_thresholds.csv`; `summary.json` | multipliers: 84=2, 126=3, 168=4 | supported |
| Low ordinary bids can make marks look more decisive than point score. | `scoreboard_distortion_examples.csv` | three barely made 30s score 90-36 points but 3-0 marks | supported |
| Tournament hand-win objectives can erase 84 multiplier value unless total marks are a tiebreaker. | `scoreboard_distortion_examples.csv`; script transform | clean 84 is 2-0 marks but only 1-0 in hand-win objective | context-limited |
| Point scoring as a better skill-honing signal is plausible but unmeasured here. | no policy population or arena | not applicable | underpowered |
| Timed advancement EV is a different objective from per-hand mark EV. | no bracket/time simulation | not applicable | not-yet-tested |

## Objective Transform Table

| bid | mark multiplier | point break-even make probability | mark break-even make probability | interpretation |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| 30 | 1 | 0.800000 | 0.500000 | point scoring heavily penalizes a low-bid set relative to a bare make |
| 35 | 1 | 0.733333 | 0.500000 | mark scoring reduces the make-threshold burden |
| 41 | 1 | 0.674797 | 0.500000 | the objectives converge near 42 |
| 42 | 1 | 0.500000 | 0.500000 | symmetric make/set objective |
| 84 | 2 | 0.500000 | 0.500000 | multiplier changes scale, not symmetric break-even |
| 126 | 3 | 0.500000 | 0.500000 | multiplier changes scale, not symmetric break-even |
| 168 | 4 | 0.500000 | 0.500000 | multiplier changes scale, not symmetric break-even |

The break-even table is a synthetic one-step utility transform, not an optimal
bidding rule. It assumes a symmetric made/set high-bid objective and uses the
terminal point formula for ordinary bids.

## Caveats

- This is scoring algebra and synthetic objective transformation only. It does
  not prove that any bid or play is optimal under [[forge]] E[Q].
- No generated game corpus was audited for score-mode coverage or tournament
  contamination.
- The point-vs-mark threshold rows are utility transforms, not a bidding model.
- Tournament objective is represented as marks or hand wins with a tiebreak hook,
  not as a timed bracket simulator.
- 84/126/168 rows validate multiplier scale and objective drift, not make
  probabilities, live 84 proof, or defender preservation policy.
