How to read it

A world is one way the tiles the mover cannot see might lie. The cell is every sampled world that agrees with the public record; Walt chooses one move for the whole cell (the plan), and plays every world out under it.

The splat of a move is where those worlds end: the bidding team's final margin, −42 to +42. A margin m means the bidders banked (42 + m)/2 points, so margins are always even and each bar is one exact score. pmake is the mass at margin ≥ +18 (the bidders bank 30); for a defender, the good side is the left one, and its number is the set chance.

Bars are coloured by which side the ending favours (made, set); the mover's side is the bright one. The white tick is the move's mean, and the strip above the splats shows the means alone: what a scalar player sees.

The rose line is how the deal really ended. It is the outcome of the line actually played, so on the chosen move it is the forecast's test; on the others it is a landmark.

The ridges below are the chosen move's splat at every planned play, each scaled to its own peak, coloured as above. Forced plays (one legal tile) have no plan and no splat.

What must be true. Every world is a whole deal, so any part of a splat has a description in holdings: which hidden tile sits with which seat. Pick a region of a move's splat (its made or set side, a hump, or drag across any bars) and the panel under the splats ranks the holdings by how far they move the region's chance: 22 of 24 where partner holds the tile against the region's base rate (the grey tick), with the rest of the cell (the hollow dot) beside it. Seats are named from the mover's chair: left plays after it, right before it. The real holder is marked, because the page knows the deal; the mover did not.

Click a holding to condition the position on it: every splat splits into the worlds where it is true (bright, at the bottom of each bar) and the rest (faint, on top), and the table gives each move's mean and pmake on both sides, starring the move that would be best if that were known. Up to three holdings stack. Every number is counted from the pour's worlds; nothing is re-planned.

The pour plans under the make utility, so q is pmake. Where the contract is already decided the plan stops and the tape plays the hand out, so margins past that point are the tape's, not the plan's (issue #107). The page shows the four real hands; the mover saw only its own: the other seats are drawn behind glass, with the share of the mover's worlds that put each tile where it really is.
